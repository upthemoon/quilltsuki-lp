Quilltsuki 2.0 — Photo Journal, Photobook, and the Friends Calendar

A journal is not just for the day you write, but for the day you look back.

Quilltsuki 2.0 is a minimal photo journal for iOS that turns daily moments into a printed photobook — and makes sharing with close friends feel physical again.

Core features

Photobook PDF export

Every entry you write becomes a page. Choose from four formats: monthly (12p), seasonal (30p), six-month (60p), or annual (120p). Export to PDF, order a print, hold the year in your hands.

Friends Calendar via Bluetooth LE

Connect with a friend code and exchange calendars only when you are physically nearby. No location data is used. Visibility expires after 24 hours. The idea is a direct response to the exhaustion of always-on social feeds.

Moon-phase design

The interface moves from new moon to full moon as the month progresses — a continuous visual rhythm rather than a daily streak counter.

Fully free

Every feature is available at no cost. Pro (¥480/month or ¥3,800/year) adds identity features — a Pro badge and zero ads.

Pioneer pass

Users who join before August 31, 2026 — or among the first 500 — receive Pro free, permanently.

Built in React Native with Expo New Architecture by Ryo, a solo iOS developer based in Japan under Up the Moon LLC.

Quilltsuki 2.0 launches for iOS in October 2026.

Design Notes

What v2.0 actually does

Three feature pillars — designed and built for slow record-keeping.

Moon-phase calendar

The synodic cycle (29.53 days) drives a subtle gradient from new moon to full moon across the month grid. As you record, your days become moons. The calendar is not a tracker — it is a record of how time passed.

Photobook PDF

Every entry becomes a page. Four formats: monthly (12p), seasonal (30p), six-month (60p), or annual (120p). Generated locally on device — no upload, no cloud round-trip. Print at year's end and hold the year in your hands.

Friends Calendar via Bluetooth LE

Exchange an 8-character friend code, then connect over Bluetooth Low Energy. When you are physically near each other, both calendars open — for 24 hours. No location data is used. It is a quieter alternative to always-on social feeds.
